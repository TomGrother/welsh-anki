Who Is It For?

Whether you're a complete beginner who's never spoken a word of Welsh, a parent learning alongside your children at a Welsh-medium school, brushing up before a Cymraeg exam or job interview, or simply reconnecting with family heritage — Dragon Lingo is built to fit around your life.

Many learners use it as a standalone course from "Bore da" through to natural conversational Welsh, while others use it as a daily vocabulary "gym" alongside a class, app or course they're already following. Just 5–10 minutes a day is enough to make steady progress thanks to spaced repetition.

🧠 How Spaced Repetition (SM-2) Works With Your Decks

▸

Every card in every deck below has its own personal schedule, tracked separately for you. When you study a deck, you're not just working through a flat list from top to bottom — you're seeing exactly the cards that are due for review right now, mixed with a handful of new cards introduced at the daily pace you chose (see Settings).

Each time you rate a card, the SM-2 algorithm decides when it should reappear:

  • Again — the card stays due today and keeps reappearing in your queue (moved to the back, so other cards come first) until you get it right. It hasn't "stuck" yet, so it won't disappear from Due Now.
  • Hard — you knew it, but it was a struggle. It comes back again fairly soon — a smaller jump than Good.
  • Good — a solid recall. The gap before its next review grows: typically 1 day → 6 days → then roughly ×2.5 each time after that (1 → 6 → ~15 → ~37 days, and so on).
  • Easy — instant recall. The gap grows even faster than Good, so well-known words fade into occasional refreshers measured in weeks or months.

This is why a deck you've "completed" can still show cards as due later — it's not re-testing everything, just the specific words that are due to resurface based on how well you knew them last time. The repetition isn't random or evenly spaced: words you find easy are repeated less and less often over time, while words you find tricky are repeated more frequently until they finally stick. Over weeks, this naturally concentrates your study time on your weak spots rather than wasting it re-confirming things you already know.
